# Related case law — ADA website cases, by the kind of barrier

These are real, widely reported cases. Each entry gives the caption, the court and year, what the plaintiff could not do on the site, and which of this auditor's checks the barrier maps to. Links go to a CourtListener search for the caption, so they resolve to the docket rather than to a page that may move. This file is background for the reader, not part of the standard; findings never cite it.

| Case | Court, year | The barrier | Maps to |
|---|---|---|---|
| Robles v. Domino's Pizza, LLC, 913 F.3d 898 | 9th Cir. 2019; Supreme Court declined review 2019 | A blind customer could not order a pizza on the website or app with a screen reader. The court held the ADA applies to the website and app of a business with physical stores. | Images without descriptions (1.1.1), controls without names (4.1.2), keyboard operation (2.1.1) |
| National Federation of the Blind v. Target Corp., 452 F. Supp. 2d 946 | N.D. Cal. 2006; settled 2008 for $6 million | Missing alt text on images, image maps that could not be read, and checkout that could not be completed without a mouse. | 1.1.1, 2.1.1, 2.4.4 |
| Gil v. Winn-Dixie Stores, Inc., 257 F. Supp. 3d 1340 | S.D. Fla. 2017 (trial verdict for plaintiff; later vacated on appeal and then dismissed as moot in 2021) | A blind customer could not refill prescriptions or load coupons because the site did not work with a screen reader. The trial court ordered WCAG 2.0 conformance. | 1.1.1, 3.3.2, 4.1.2 |
| Andrews v. Blick Art Materials, LLC, 268 F. Supp. 3d 381 | E.D.N.Y. 2017 | A blind shopper could not use the retailer's website; the court held a commercial website is itself a place of public accommodation in that district. | General: any Level A failure |
| National Association of the Deaf v. Netflix, Inc., 869 F. Supp. 2d 196 | D. Mass. 2012; settled with a captioning schedule | Streaming video without captions. The court allowed the ADA claim against a web-only service. | Video captions (1.2.2) |
| National Association of the Deaf v. Harvard University | D. Mass., filed 2015, settled 2019 | Online lectures and public video without accurate captions. | 1.2.2 |

## Volume

Federal website accessibility suits under ADA Title III: 3,117 filed in 2025, about 36 percent of all Title III federal filings that year, with trackers projecting more than 5,000 in 2026 including state courts. Sources: [Level Access](https://www.levelaccess.com/blog/2024-u-s-web-accessibility-litigation-key-trends-and-strategies-for-mitigating-risk/), [Abledly](https://abledly.com/ada-lawsuit-statistics), [UserWay](https://userway.org/blog/web-accessibility-lawsuit/). The three barriers named most often in complaints are missing image descriptions, unlabeled form fields, and pages that cannot be used with a keyboard ([Accessible.org](https://accessible.org/lawsuits/)).
